THE NEXT ROUND

The NCAA tournament started back in 1939, and 40 years later they started seeding teams. In over 30+ years of seeding teams, only 3 teams seeded lower than no. 4 seed have ever won the Championship. Villanova as the 8 seed in 1985, was the lowest seeded team to ever win the tournament. Jim Valvano and NC State team won in 1983, and Danny Mannings Kansas team in 1988 both as no. 6 seeds were the only other and lowest seeded teams to cut down the nets. Going by that fact, you can pretty much just scratch off 12 teams in each region, especially going into the second and third round in the tournament. Play-in games notwithstanding. In fact only 8 teams seeded higher than no. 6 have ever reached the final four! Taking an underdog, especially one lower than say a 5 seed, to WIN a region is a sucker bet.

WHERE TO FIND THE UPSETS

That all being said, this is the NCAA tournament, there are celebrated upsets every year, the Trick is to find them. Pretty much since the inception of this tournament when they started seeding teams there has been a 5 seed upset a number 12 seed every single year. In fact this has become even more common here in recent years, we have seen numerous tournaments when 2 or more 12 seeds all pull of that first round upset.
Now there's a couple of reasons for that, As we just told you above, the top four teams rarely if ever lose. Since there are going to be upsets every year the best place to go and pick one, is a 12-5 match up. And who knows if the committee doesn't have a hand in perpetuating that by overseeding some number 5 seeds against more underrated 12 teams. Look especially for the big name conference program, the marquee name, more than likely not having a typical year for them, a team close to a .500 record, going against A lesser renowned school with a quality body of work behind them, such as a mid-major champion, or a smaller conference school that just dominated with only two or three losses on the year. It is different teams every year, that is the great thing about college ball. Yet in these 12-5s you might also get a big name young program with talented freshmen and sophomores going against that mid-major champion laden with seniors. Another situation where the potential upset could lurk.
Whatever the reason there has been a 12-5 upset every year, and some years we might have more than one 12-5 upset. So in any case you wouldn't be wanting of having No 5 seeds going very deep. Just in case they don't get out of that first round. And in the case of Cinderella, or a 12 seed, again they were a 12 seed for a reason, so while picking one or two in round one, you don't want to get too attached hoping that lightning strikes the same spot twice. So right there that gives you another 4 picks there going on into the next round of picks...
